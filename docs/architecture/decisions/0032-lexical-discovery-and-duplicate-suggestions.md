# 0032. Lexical procedure discovery by task, and advisory duplicate suggestions

**Status:** Accepted. Builds on [ADR-0020](0020-procedure-origin-and-applicability.md), [ADR-0021](0021-targeted-feedback-and-bounded-lists.md) and [ADR-0023](0023-pagination-guarantees-and-scope-labels.md).
**Date:** 2026-10-11

## Context

Agents find procedures by listing them, filtered by `repository`, `scope` and `q`, a substring of the canonical key or goal ([ADR-0021](0021-targeted-feedback-and-bounded-lists.md)). A task worded differently finds nothing, method, philosophy, contract and instructions are never searched, and many versions have no goal. Agents then write procedures that already exist, including reusable subprocedures, and nothing tells them so ([#79](https://github.com/ashuangiras/polaroid/issues/79)). Polaroid must supply candidates and explain them; the agent judges relevance and decides whether to reuse, revise or create. The first increment uses no model, embedding service, vector store or new dependency, so it is lexical: it matches words, not meaning.

## Decision

- **Terms.** Text is lowercased and split at every character that is not a Unicode letter or digit. Words shorter than two characters and a fixed list of common English function words are dropped. Each remaining word becomes a term by removing at most one suffix, the first that applies, when at least three characters remain: `ies`/`ied` → `y`, then `ing`, `ed`, `es`, `s` (not after `s`), `e`. So `dependencies` and `dependency`, `publishing` and `publish`, `migrate` and `migrating` meet. Explanations report the words as the request wrote them.
- **What is searched.** Each procedure's **latest version** only, at the time of the read: the canonical key (split at its separators), `goal`, `method`, `philosophy`, and the string values of `contract` and `instructions` at any depth. Member names, numbers, IDs, timestamps, `revision_reason`, references, origins, bindings and executions are never searched. Every procedure is a candidate on its own, so a procedure used as a subprocedure is found like any other.
- **Candidates and visibility.** With `repository` (an identifier, registered or not), candidates are the procedures whose latest version is applicable there: shared, unspecified, or local to its registered repository, matched by identity as list filters are ([ADR-0022](0022-repository-identity-in-evidence.md)). Procedures local to other repositories are excluded. Without `repository`, the whole catalog is searched, local procedures included, each labelled with its `scope` and `applicability`; the catalog is shared, and scope is not access control.
- **Discovery** (`GET /v1/procedures/discovery?task=…[&repository=…][&limit=…]`, MCP `discover_procedures`, CLI `discover`). `task` is required, at most 4096 bytes, and must contain at least one term. Fields weigh 3 (canonical key, goal), 2 (method) or 1 (philosophy, contract, instructions). For each candidate:
  - `matched_terms` is how many of the task's distinct terms occur anywhere in it;
  - `score` is the sum, over those terms, of the highest weight among the fields containing each;
  - `matches` lists, per field in that order, the task's words found there.

  Candidates matching at least one term are ranked by `score`, then `matched_terms`, both descending, then by canonical key, which is unique, so ties are stable. Ranking by score first keeps a term in a key or goal ahead of incidental words in a long instruction document. `limit` is 1 to 50, default 10; `matched` reports how many procedures matched before the limit. There are no cursors: a ranked result is refined by rewording the task, not paged. Each candidate names its procedure, canonical key, the `version` searched, `scope`, `applicability`, `goal` when present and `method` as the description; agents fetch the version for its contract and instructions.
- **Duplicate suggestions** (`POST /v1/procedures/duplicates`, MCP `suggest_duplicates`, CLI `duplicates`). Read-only; the body is a proposal: `method` and `philosophy` (required, as in a version), optional `goal` and `canonical_key`, and optional `repository`, `exclude_procedure_id` and `limit` (1 to 50, default 10).
  - **Key collision:** when `canonical_key` names an existing procedure, other than the excluded one, it is reported as `key_collision`. That is the existing identity rule: creating it would get `409 canonical_key_exists`. It is reported whatever the repository, because keys are unique across the catalog.
  - **Content overlap** is advisory. The proposal's terms from canonical key, goal, method and philosophy are compared with the same fields of each candidate's latest version. `similarity` is the Dice coefficient, 2 × shared terms ÷ (proposal terms + candidate terms), shown to two decimals; a candidate is suggested when it is at least 0.35, compared exactly. Contract and instructions are left out because procedures of one domain share their procedural vocabulary. The threshold was set on Polaroid's own catalog of 16 procedures (120 distinct pairs) before any demonstration: with a prototype of these rules, distinct procedures reached 0.66 with contract and instructions and at most 0.48 without them (median 0.20), while every revision of one procedure scored at least 0.66 against its first version. With the implementation, 6 of the 120 distinct pairs reach 0.35 (at most 0.48), so a suggestion stays rare and remains a prompt to read, not a verdict. Suggestions are ranked by similarity, then shared terms, then canonical key, and explain the shared words per field.
  - Candidates follow the discovery rules for `repository`, so shared procedures that could be reused instead of a local copy are included. `exclude_procedure_id` (for a revision) must name an existing procedure.
  - The operation never stores, merges, rejects, revises or binds anything, and never changes how creation is checked.
- **Discovery is not resolution or verification.** It reads only the latest versions' text, never executions, bindings or evidence. A candidate's version may be unverified everywhere, and resolution may select another version by evidence ([ADR-0013](0013-evidence-based-resolution.md)). Nothing about selection, verification or listing changes. Both operations read in one statement and need no migration.

## Consequences

- An agent can describe its task, get a short, explained list, read the candidates it judges relevant, and check a proposal before `create_procedure` or `revise_procedure`.
- Matching is lexical. Synonyms without a shared word ("compile" and "build") do not meet, frequent domain words raise unrelated candidates, and a long instruction document matches incidental words; scores have no meaning across different tasks. The suffix rule merges some unrelated words and misses irregular forms. Semantic matching would need a new ADR and, likely, a dependency.
- Each request reads every candidate's latest version: linear in the catalog's size, which suits a per-user catalog. An index can come later without changing the contract.
- New endpoints, tools and commands only; existing requests, records and the schema are unchanged.
